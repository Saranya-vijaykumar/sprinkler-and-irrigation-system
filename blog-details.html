The Homeowner’s Guide to Sprinkler Winterization: Why Skipping Blowout Destroys Manifolds

Tyler Vance, CID
• October 12, 2026 • 7 min read

1. The Physics of Water Expansion in Subterranean PVC

When liquid water drops below 32°F (0°C), its molecular matrix crystallizes into hexagonal ice structures, causing a volume expansion of roughly 9%. In an enclosed subterranean PVC pipe or brass backflow preventer, this creates thousands of pounds per square inch of outward hydraulic pressure.

Even in areas where the soil only freezes 4 to 6 inches deep, sprinkler lateral lines often sit above the regional frost line. If water remains inside the valves, the plastic solenoid housing will hairline fracture, resulting in expensive geysers upon spring startup.

Contractor Warning

Never use excessive pressure over 50 PSI when blowing out polyethylene lines. It is high volume (CFM) that clears lines safely, not high pressure, which can melt internal gears or blow sprinkler heads out of their risers.

2. Why Shop Vacuums and Small Home Compressors Fail

A standard pancake air compressor found in home garages produces high pressure (120 PSI) but only 2 to 4 CFM (Cubic Feet per Minute) of air volume. Sprinkler systems require massive air volume—typically 80 to 185 CFM—to push a solid wall of water out of the lines without leaving pockets that settle at low elevation bends.

3. The 4 Essential Steps of Commercial High-CFM Blowout

  • Step 1: Shut off main water isolation valve and isolate the backflow preventer.
  • Step 2: Connect tow-behind commercial compressor to blowout port regulated strictly to 45-50 PSI.
  • Step 3: Cycle zones individually from furthest to nearest until only fine mist exits the nozzle heads.
  • Step 4: Leave test cocks and manifold bleed screws at 45-degree angles to allow freeze expansion.
